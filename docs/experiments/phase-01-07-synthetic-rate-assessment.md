# Phase 1.07 Synthetic-Rate Assessment

## Result

The canonical Inforsight generator remains an engineering coverage fixture, not a calibrated portfolio. Its 100-policy run contains exactly 25 active, 25 recovered, 25 lapsed, and 25 surrendered histories by construction. No public measure reviewed here is sufficiently comparable to justify changing those weights, the generator, or the published sample.

The assessment therefore makes four decisions:

1. Retain the equal scenario allocation as a deterministic coverage fixture.
2. Consider separately configurable scenario weights only when a later observation, robustness, or modeling consumer defines a concrete need.
3. Defer annual lapse or surrender calibration until the simulator has multi-period exposure, policy duration, and product definitions that support a compatible denominator.
4. Retain current premium, billing, payment, contact, and timing distributions as disclosed fictional inputs rather than calling them calibrated.

This result does not establish actuarial credibility, insurer representativeness, market prevalence, record-level realism, fairness, causal validity, or predictive utility.

### Payment metrics

The corpus contains 125 payment-attempt events: 50 failures and 75 receipts. The event-level failure proportion is therefore 40%, and the receipt proportion is 60%. A recovered policy has a failed payment followed by a received payment, so the denominator is payment events rather than unique policies or billing items.

These values are generated by the four scenario branches. They are not delinquency, collection, or premium-persistency estimates.

## Public source register

### SOA Research Institute and LIMRA term/whole-life experience

- **Source:** [2015–2022 Term and Whole Life Insurance Policy Surrender/Lapse Experience Study Report](https://www.soa.org/globalassets/assets/files/resources/experience-studies/2024/15-22-twlls.pdf)
- **Published:** December 2024
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-18
- **Relevant locations:** pages 1–2 for scope, population, totals, and the combined lapse definition; page 4 for methodology and limitations.
- **Population:** fully underwritten, single-life term and whole-life policies sold in the United States and its territories, contributed by 28 companies. Workplace, simplified-issue, guaranteed-issue, joint-life, and non-U.S. business are excluded.
- **Period:** seven complete study years, 2016–2022, spanning policy anniversaries in 2015–2022.
- **Exposure basis:** policy exposure calculated using the Balducci approach; the report also discusses face-amount exposure.
- **Headline totals:** approximately 135.9 million policy exposures and 5.4 million surrenders and lapses.
- **Definition:** the report uses “lapse” to include terminations with value, meaning surrenders, and terminations without value, meaning forfeitures.

Dividing the two rounded headline totals produces an approximate termination-to-exposure indicator of 3.9735%. The source does not present that derived value as an official aggregate lapse rate, and the detailed segmented results are outside the public report. This assessment records the derived figure only to make the scale and denominator mismatch visible; it is not a calibration target.

The report also states that more than 60% of count exposure is whole life. That is an exposure mix across participating business, not an issuance count, and its plan definitions do not map to Inforsight's randomly assigned fictional product labels.

### SOA experience-study calculation guidance

- **Source:** [Experience Study Calculations Educational Tool](https://www.soa.org/resources/tables-calcs-tools/experience-study-tool/)
- **Materials:** report originally updated in June 2023; linked materials shown as updated March 2024
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-18
- **Relevant location:** page introduction describing experience-rate calculations and errors created by partial rate years.

This is a methodological reference rather than a portfolio benchmark. It supports an exposure-aware rate design and the decision not to manufacture an annual rate from a single generated path. It supplies no calibration target.

## Compatibility matrix

| Synthetic measure | Public measure | Classification | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 terminal outcomes / 100 generated policies | Approximate 5.4 million surrenders and lapses / 135.9 million policy exposures | **Not comparable** | Inforsight forces one scenario path per policy and has no policy-year exposure. The source spans complete study years, applies exposure methodology, combines surrender and forfeiture, and reflects duration and product effects absent from the simulator. |
| 51 fictional term and 49 fictional whole-life issuance labels | More than 60% whole-life exposure by count | **Not comparable** | Fictional labels are random contract inputs and do not map to study plan codes. Issuance counts and multi-year exposure counts have different denominators. |
| One-cycle policy and event proportions | Experience-rate calculation methods | **Directional only** | The methodology explains why exposure and complete rate periods matter. It supports non-annualization but gives no numerical target. |

No comparison qualifies as directly comparable. Missing fields and exposure are evidence of non-comparability, not permission to invent adjustments.

## Calibration decisions

| Assumption | Disposition | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equal active, recovered, lapsed, and surrendered allocation | **Retain as fixture** | Preserve deterministic coverage and keep the 25% values labeled as engineered proportions. |
| Scenario weights configurable separately from the canonical fixture | **Parameterize later** | Add only when a later consumer defines the population, horizon, and purpose. Do not destabilize the acceptance corpus now. |
| Annual lapse or surrender calibration | **Defer pending contract support** | Requires multi-period exposure, policy duration, clearer product definitions, and a compatible external denominator. |
| Premium, billing-frequency, payment, contact, and timing distributions | **Retain as fixture** | They exercise contracts and paths; this assessment found no compatible public target. |
| Generator, schema, or published sample changes | **No change** | No comparable evidence justifies a versioned data change in Phase 1.07. |

## Implications for Phase 2

Phase 2 may use the generator to demonstrate observation construction, leakage prevention, temporal mechanics, and reproducible evaluation code. It must not present synthetic model metrics as evidence of real-world performance.

Before defining the 90-day label, Phase 2.01 must decide how a 90-day observation horizon relates to the current one-path generator and must preserve lapse and surrender as distinct structured outcomes even if a later bounded experiment combines them. If statistical scenario weights are introduced, they should be separate from the canonical coverage corpus and carry explicit provenance.

## Limitations and claim boundary

- The corpus has 100 policies and four forced scenarios.
- Each policy has one billing item; recovered histories have a second payment attempt.
- Policies do not accumulate multi-year exposure.
- Policy duration, issue age, face amount, acquisition channel, richer product definitions, and reinstatement are absent.
- Timing rules and values are invented engineering inputs.
- Public source totals are rounded and detailed results are not part of the public report.
- Aggregate agreement, if it existed, would not validate individual histories or subgroups.
- This is generator-assumption assessment, not Phase 2 probability calibration.

The assessment uses original repository code, fictional structured records, and public aggregate sources only. It commits no copied public dataset, customer information, insurer-confidential material, credentials, or proprietary procedures.
